# Interview prep: industry notes to review

Written Nov 20, 2026. These notes shape the practice questions and the opening for each kind of role. **Approved industries are live for visitors. The rest are drafts, so only the owner's test builds get them.** Approved Nov 20, 2026 by Juston Warthen: Sales, Administrative and office, Healthcare front office, Nursing and clinical care, Skilled trades and maintenance, Driving and logistics, Warehouse and production, Retail and hospitality, Teaching and education, Accounting and finance, Customer service, Software and IT, Management and supervision, Marketing and content, Human resources and recruiting.

How to review: read one industry, fix anything that is wrong or that you would not say, and tell Claude which ones are approved. Approved ones are switched on for visitors with your name and the date. Anyone who has worked in that field is the best reviewer. Nothing here states a fact about a person or a company, and no numbers appear.

What to look for: (1) Are these the things interviewers in this field really test? (2) Would the personal-moment ideas feel natural and true to a person in this field? (3) Is the voice right?

## Sales

Job titles that match: sales, account executive, account manager, business development, sdr, bdr, inside sales, outside sales, territory manager.

- **Measured on:** quota attainment; pipeline built; meetings or demos booked; conversion between stages; average deal size; sales cycle length; activity volume.
- **Exercises interviewers use:** walk through a discovery call from the opening to the agreed next step; a sell-me-this or pitch-the-product exercise; handling a common objection such as price or timing; a mock cold call or outreach message; how they would plan a territory or a list of accounts.
- **Real situations of the work:** a prospect who goes quiet; a deal lost to a competitor; a stalled deal that needs a push; handing a closed deal to the team that delivers it.
- **Rules, privacy, or safety topics:** honest claims about the product; do-not-call and email consent rules where they apply; accurate records in the CRM.
- **Opening: lead the proof with:** a result that moved a number, such as leads followed up, meetings booked, deals won, or goals met, said as one plain result.
- **Opening: personal moments that can bridge to the work:**
  - a first job where they had to earn a stranger's trust, which can speak to opening conversations
  - a time they kept going after hearing no, which can speak to follow-up and staying with a deal
  - growing up around a family business or selling something small, which can speak to understanding what a customer cares about
- **Opening: voice:** direct, warm, and specific; confident without bragging; never salesy.

## Administrative and office support

Job titles that match: administrative, executive assistant, office manager, office coordinator, receptionist, secretary, clerk, data entry.

- **Measured on:** accuracy of the work; turnaround on requests; scheduling conflicts resolved; supplies and vendor costs kept in line; response time to messages and calls.
- **Exercises interviewers use:** prioritize several competing requests from different people right now; a scheduling puzzle with conflicts; write or proofread a short professional email; how they would organize a messy shared folder or process; what they do when someone asks for information that is confidential; how they decide what to handle themselves and what to bring to the person they support.
- **Real situations of the work:** two leaders who each want priority; a last-minute change to travel or a meeting; an error found after something was sent; an upset caller or visitor; overhearing or seeing something private; a temporary assignment that ends and the handoff to the next person.
- **Rules, privacy, or safety topics:** confidentiality of what they see and hear; handling sensitive documents; access to private calendars and email.
- **Opening: lead the proof with:** keeping things running: schedules handled, requests turned around, records kept right, people supported.
- **Opening: personal moments that can bridge to the work:**
  - being the person at home or in a group who keeps everyone organized, which can speak to coordination
  - a past job where small mistakes mattered, which can speak to accuracy
  - helping a relative or a small family business with paperwork, which can speak to being trusted with private details
- **Opening: voice:** calm, organized, and plain; reliability shown through one specific example, never just claimed.

## Healthcare front office and billing

Job titles that match: patient access, patient registration, medical receptionist, medical office, medical secretary, medical biller, medical billing, medical coder, patient scheduler, unit clerk, insurance verification.

- **Measured on:** accuracy of registration and insurance information; claims rejected or denied because of front-end errors; copays collected at check-in; check-in time and patient wait; no-shows and how full the schedule is.
- **Exercises interviewers use:** a patient arrives with missing or wrong insurance information, what are the steps; explain a cost or copay to a patient clearly and kindly; verify details on a described record and catch what does not match.
- **Real situations of the work:** an upset patient who has been waiting; a patient in pain or distress at the desk; a caller asking about another person's information; a double-booked schedule; the system going down at a busy time.
- **Rules, privacy, or safety topics:** HIPAA privacy: who may be told what, and checking identity first; sharing only the minimum needed; accurate, complete patient records.
- **Opening: lead the proof with:** patients checked in, records or billing handled correctly, calls and scheduling kept moving.
- **Opening: personal moments that can bridge to the work:**
  - time spent in a waiting room or caring for a relative, which can speak to how patients feel at the front desk
  - a past job where privacy mattered, which can speak to handling sensitive information
  - being the calm one when others are stressed, which can speak to patients who are worried
- **Opening: voice:** warm, careful, and steady; privacy and accuracy matter more than speed.

- **Measured on:** work done right the first time, without callbacks; jobs finished on schedule; safety record; accuracy of material and time estimates; customer feedback on site.

- **Rules, privacy, or safety topics:** safety rules and protective equipment; lockout and tagout where it applies; local codes and inspections; licenses and certifications they actually hold.
- **Opening: lead the proof with:** work done and done right: jobs finished, what was built or fixed, safety habits, and working on a crew.
- **Opening: personal moments that can bridge to the work:**
  - growing up around a shop or a family business, or fixing things at home, which can speak to hands-on comfort and doing it right the first time
  - a job where carelessness cost something, which can speak to safety and checking their work
  - learning from an older worker, which can speak to taking direction and wanting to learn the trade
- **Opening: voice:** plain and practical in short sentences; pride in the work shown through detail, not adjectives.
